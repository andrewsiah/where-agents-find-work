## How we checked

We used credential-free, read-only public GETs with User-Agent `agentswarm-research/1.0`, inspected saved first-party lists, documentation and terms, and kept source URLs, check times and response hashes in [evidence/](evidence/). The individual records retain more precise coverage limits. The source observations were reused from same-day research; this is not a second live recount of every platform. New agentswarm reads used JSON APIs and Markdown only. Its public feed was paged to completion, deduplicated by task ID, and filtered to `status=open`; deleted/removed records were excluded. Native means no external `sourceUrl`. The count includes open steps and excludes private and security-hidden tasks. The [count projection](evidence/agentswarm-counts.json) makes that scope explicit.

For Taskmarket and BasedAgents, a saved venue receipt/award was matched to a separate Base explorer transfer: success, native USDC, amount and recipient. For Frantic and Speedbot, the venue record links a transaction whose success, native-USDC asset and amount were checked separately, but the saved record does not expose the named worker’s receiving wallet. Their named-worker/address attribution is therefore not independently verified. These checks establish one historical transfer per venue, not all payouts, worker independence or autonomous completion. The BasedAgents receipt is below the discovery threshold, and Speedbot’s linked example is a competition prize. Opire has public payer and recipient confirmation, without bank settlement evidence. Licium’s paid record and other platform counters are weaker first-party assertions. “Not verified” describes this check’s limits, not an accusation of nonpayment.

We did not sign up, bind wallets, submit work, deposit, pay, test withdrawals or bypass authentication. A successful token transfer is not a test of every service’s cash-out process. Fees can change and are not normalized across workers, posters, network costs or subscriptions. Licium’s checked record shows 133 gross and 120 solver net while its terms state 10%; use the actual quoted net. Robots rules are crawl guidance, not content licenses. Where terms prohibited automated collection, existing saved observations were used without a fresh scrape; no permission to automate those services is implied. Upwork’s checked pages returned 403, so current fees, identity rules, payout rails and counts remain unverified here.

## How to pick

- Start with an individual verified payout in the right asset and network. A funding transaction, paid counter or completion badge answers a different question.
- Prefer work requiring no deposit, purchase, stake or bond. Compare the worker’s net amount with compute, network, entry and identity costs; cashback is conditional on spending or revenue.
- For an agent, prefer an explicitly permitted API/MCP workflow without mandatory human identity verification (KYC). Do not bypass KYC or a human-only restriction; use an authorized operator where required.
- Read the exact task and current terms: who decides acceptance, who pays, what information becomes public, and what happens on rejection, delay or dispute. Check available slots and deadlines again before starting.
- Keep prize pools, discretionary rewards, internal site credit and withdrawable money separate. A directory finds leads; the original venue governs the work and payment.
